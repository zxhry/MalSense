In short

  • MalSense is an educational tool, built as a final-year project at USIM. It helps you understand Android malware.
  • Its results can be wrong. Use them as advice, not as a guarantee, and do not rely on MalSense alone to protect your phone.
  • Only upload files you are allowed to share, and never use MalSense to harm anyone.

1. What MalSense is

MalSense checks Android app files (APKs) for signs of malware. An AI model (XGBoost) studies the permissions and structure of the app, VirusTotal's antivirus engines double-check the file's fingerprint, and MalSense explains the result in plain language. It also offers security-awareness quizzes, a Mobile Security Checklist and Digital Amanah guidance. By using MalSense you agree to these terms and to the Privacy Policy.

2. Results are guidance, not a guarantee

  • MalSense uses static analysis only – it reads the app without running it, so it cannot see everything an app does once installed.
  • The AI model is about 97% accurate on its test data, which means some malware can be missed (a false negative) and some safe apps can be flagged (a false positive).
  • MalSense is not a replacement for official app stores, Google Play Protect or professional security tools. You decide whether to install an app, and you are responsible for that decision.

3. Using MalSense responsibly

You agree that you will not:

  • upload files you do not have the right to share, or files containing other people's personal data;
  • use MalSense, its reports or its quiz content to create, improve or spread malware, or to harm any person or system;
  • try to break, overload or get around the website's security (for example automated mass uploads);
  • create accounts with false details or use someone else's account.

4. Accounts

  • You can scan as a guest. An account (email and password, or Google) adds scan history and downloadable reports.
  • Keep your password private. You are responsible for activity on your account.
  • The administrator may deactivate accounts that break these terms.

5. Reporting APKs

When you report an APK, you allow the administrator to keep it in quarantine, analyse it, and use it to retrain and improve the AI model. Do not install a suspicious app to report it – just send the file.

6. Outside services

MalSense uses VirusTotal (antivirus results), Google Gemini (quiz questions) and Google sign-in. These services have their own terms, and their availability and limits are outside MalSense's control – for example, a VirusTotal result may be missing when the free daily limit is reached.

7. AI and generated content

  • MalSense uses two kinds of AI: an XGBoost model that classifies an APK as malware or benign, and Google Gemini, which writes quiz questions from the findings of a scan.
  • Both can be wrong. Quiz questions are AI-generated and may contain mistakes or be unclear. Check anything important with a trusted source.
  • The results, explanations, advice and quizzes are for learning. They are not professional security, legal or religious advice. If you think your phone is infected or you may have lost money, contact your bank or a qualified professional.

8. Content and credits

  • Antivirus results come from VirusTotal. Attacker techniques are described using MITRE ATT&CK for Mobile. The AI model was trained on the KronoDroid dataset (Guerra-Manzanares, Bahsi and Nõmm, 2021).
  • Quran verses on the Digital Amanah page follow the Sahih International English translation. Digital Amanah gives general reminders that encourage safe digital habits. It is not a religious ruling (fatwa). For religious questions, please ask a qualified teacher or scholar.
